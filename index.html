How it works

Slouch, and the screen dims. Sit up, and it clears.

No pop-ups, no sounds, no charts to read. Your screen simply gets harder to look at until you sit the way you meant to.

Breaks

Sitting too long is the other half.

After 50 minutes in your chair, Spino asks you to stand up and counts down the minute you spend away. You choose the interval, from 3 to 120 minutes. When the minute is up, the break counts and the timer starts over.

Out of your way

Lives in the menu bar. Nowhere else.

No Dock icon, no windows

One small dinosaur next to the clock. Click it to see how you are sitting today.

Pauses on its own

Step away, lock the screen or close the lid, and the camera turns off until you are back.

Snooze or pause any time

Quiet for 30 minutes, or off completely, camera included.

Handy extras, if you want them

Dim the screen at night with ⌃⌥D, keep the Mac awake, or put the display to sleep in one click.

Privacy

The camera image never leaves your Mac.

Not a promise. A property of the app, enforced by macOS.

Processed in memory

Every frame is analysed on your Mac with Apple's Vision framework, then dropped. Nothing is recorded or saved.

No network at all

Spino runs in the App Sandbox with the camera as its only permission. There is no networking code to begin with.

You always know

A green dot shows exactly when the camera is on.

No account, no tracking

No sign-up, no analytics. The only thing stored is your calibration: a handful of numbers.

Install

Running in a minute.

Spino is not in the App Store yet, so macOS asks you to approve it once.

Drag to Applications

Open Spino.dmg and drag Spino into Applications.

Open it once

macOS says it cannot verify the developer. Click Done.

Allow it

System Settings → Privacy & Security, scroll down, click Open Anyway.

Allow the camera

Spino appears in the menu bar. Grant camera access and calibrate.

On macOS 14 Sonoma you can skip step 3: right-click Spino in Applications, choose Open, then Open again. Once per version.
